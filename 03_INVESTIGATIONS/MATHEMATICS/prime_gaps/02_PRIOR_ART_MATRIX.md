# EXP-0008 Prior-Art and Novelty Matrix

**Purpose:** identify what is already known, what is equivalent, and what (if anything) would be genuinely new in the EXP-0008 package. “Related” does not mean identical: the key distinction is between a named published statistic, a standard mathematical model, and a finite-range numerical protocol.

## Additional direct prior art and dependence results

The independent literature audit also identified the following particularly relevant sources:

- **Odlyzko–Rubinstein–Wolf (1999), “Jumping champions,”** *Experimental Mathematics* 8, 107–118, DOI [10.1080/10586458.1999.10504393](https://doi.org/10.1080/10586458.1999.10504393): frequencies of exact even gap values and their arithmetic modulation. This is direct prior art for raw gap-frequency patterns, although not the EXP-0008 ten-bin test.
- **Wolf (2011/2018), arXiv:1102.0481:** heuristic fixed-gap counts using singular-series and consecutive-prime corrections. This is the model family that EXP-0008’s raw `Exp(1)` null omits.
- **Pintz (2014), arXiv:1406.2658:** proves extreme ratios of consecutive gaps (`liminf=0`, `limsup=infinity`). This is direct evidence that adjacent gaps cannot be treated as a simple i.i.d. sequence.
- **Banks–Freiberg–Turnage-Butterbaugh (2013/14), arXiv:1311.7003:** constructs arbitrarily long increasing, decreasing, and divisibility-constrained runs of consecutive gaps. This supports higher-order dependence concerns.
- **Montgomery–Soundararajan (2004), “Primes in short intervals,”** DOI [10.1007/s00220-004-1222-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00220-004-1222-4), arXiv:math/0409258: gives a different mesoscopic prime-counting model and illustrates why a smooth Cramér approximation can depend on scale.
- **Banks–Freiberg–Maynard (2014),** “On limit points of the sequence of normalized prime gaps,” DOI [10.1112/plms/pdw036](https://doi.org/10.1112/plms/pdw036), arXiv:1404.5094: directly studies the exact normalized sequence \((p_{n+1}-p_n)/\log p_n\).

The independent search did not verify the attribution “Conrey–Goldston–Keating” used in the project’s `QUESTION.md` as a single identifiable prime-gap source. The relevant verified anchors are Gallagher, Goldston–Ledoan, Goldston–Pintz–Yıldırım, Wolf, and the later residue-bias literature. This attribution should be corrected or qualified.

## Exact-protocol search result

No paper was found that publishes the exact combination of:

1. lower-prime normalization by `log(p)`;
2. four decade blocks `[10^4,10^5)`, `[10^5,10^6)`, `[10^6,10^7)`, `[10^7,10^8)`;
3. ten exponential-quantile bins;
4. the exact C1–C7/BH/bootstrap decision tree; and
5. the Q-M007 40-cell reanalysis.

This is a **protocol-level distinction**, not a novelty finding. A unique combination of known operations can still be scientifically ordinary. No exact protocol match is required to classify a result as non-novel when its dominant behavior is already explained by parity and established model limitations.

## Literature conclusion

The **scientific object** is old. The **exact implementation recipe** is not a standard named theorem, but the observed mismatch is not evidence of a new law. The novelty question can only become live after a new experiment demonstrates a residual against a justified wheel/singular-series model, with dependence-aware uncertainty, independent implementation, and a literature review of that residual.

## Search method and limitations

Searches included exact-title/author queries, DOI/Crossref results, arXiv HTML, publisher pages, and references in the project and in Cohen/Wolf papers. Sources were not treated as instructions. Search results can miss unpublished, non-indexed, or differently formulated work. The matrix therefore distinguishes “not found” from “does not exist.”
